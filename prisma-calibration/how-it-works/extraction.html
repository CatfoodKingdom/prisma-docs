Extraction

Extraction

Jul 26, 20265 min read

Extraction turns one photograph into color measurements. Input: a source photo of a printed strip on a lightbox, the matching blank photo (same lightbox, no strip), and the strip’s known geometry. RAW capture is the intended evidence path, although the extraction helpers also accept supported raster formats. Output: per-swatch transmission — the fraction of the lightbox’s light each thickness step passes, per color channel.

Two image domains

Extraction uses a display version — for RAW input, the camera-processed, auto-brightened rendering — to locate the strip and show humans. A linear version, with no tone curve or brightness adjustment, keeps pixel values proportional to light and is used for measurement. The automatic path first tries a cheap embedded preview for detection and later produces full-resolution visual and linear arrays; this is not a promise that each file is opened or decoded exactly twice. Transmission is only meaningful in the linear domain; see Transmission vs Appearance.

Locating the strip

The detector first looks for the lightbox — the large bright region of the photo — and searches inside it when found. If no lightbox is detected, it searches the full frame; the final pure-geometry fallback is available only when a lightbox rectangle exists. Detection strategies run in fixed order, each proposing candidate outlines that are screened against the strip’s expected proportions, computed from its geometry:

  1. difference from the blank (per-channel ratio, then absolute difference);
  2. dark-shape-on-bright thresholding, then the reverse;
  3. edge detection;
  4. color saturation, for translucent tinted filaments the grayscale strategies miss;
  5. a pure-geometry fallback that places the expected rectangle at the lightbox center.

First success wins.

Alignment

Three corrections put both images in a canonical frame. The photo is oriented upright (a per-photo rotation plus the sample’s recorded open-side direction). The detected strip is rotated to horizontal and cropped. It is then flipped, if needed, so the thinnest step sits on the left. On the automatic path that flip is decided by brightness, since thin steps transmit more light; on the manual path (below) the hand-clicked corner order and the sample’s recorded open-side orientation fix the direction, so no brightness flip is applied. The blank is warped onto the sample photo by matching the lightbox positions in each, then carried through the same rotation, crop, and flip, so strip and blank align pixel for pixel.

The measurement

Per channel, each pixel’s transmission is the ratio of the light that arrived through the strip to the light the lightbox delivered at that same spot:

Tc​(x,y)=clip(Bc​(x,y)Sc​(x,y)​, 0, 1)

where S is the linear strip photo and B the registered linear blank. This division is the flatfield correction: it cancels the lightbox’s spatial unevenness.

Swatch regions come from geometry alone: the strip’s known millimeter dimensions place the border, the equal-width steps, and a small inset at the open edge, where over-extrusion distorts the print. No image signal adjusts the dividers. Within each step, a central patch spanning half the cell’s width and height is sampled, and the swatch’s per-channel value is the median of its pixels’ transmissions — a median of ratios, robust to dust and local defects. The display-domain median is stored alongside for on-screen swatches, and the blank’s own median brightness is recorded for traceability; it is never divided again.

Appearance capture

When embedded-JPEG appearance extraction succeeds, the result also records how the camera rendered each swatch. Rather than re-detect the strip inside that preview, it reuses the location the transmission extraction already settled on: the embedded JPEG is rotated and scaled into that same coordinate frame, and the swatch patches are sampled at the matching positions. (An independent detection of the strip within the JPEG remains as a fallback for when that recorded location is unavailable.) Failure is recorded in diagnostics and leaves appearance absent without invalidating transmission. These camera-rendered colors are not light measurements; they are the fitting target for The Camera Transform.

Manual fallback

When automatic detection fails, the strip’s four corners are clicked by hand (Manual Processing). The quad is rectified with a full perspective warp — the automatic path uses rotation only — its direction is fixed by the hand-clicked corner order and the recorded open-side orientation rather than by the brightness flip, and blank registration must succeed exactly rather than fall back to an approximation. From the flatfield division onward, the measurement is identical.

Outcomes

A successful committed run stores one current extraction record per sample: every swatch’s transmission, display, and appearance colors, plus provenance for how the strip was located and diagnostic scores. An ordinary detection or pipeline failure before replacement persists failed and discards an earlier result so it cannot masquerade as new output. A failure inside the atomic replacement commit instead rolls back to the prior sample/result pair, and a process stop after SQLite commits is converged with the journaled visual pair at startup. Noncommitting preview runs do not change the durable result. Whether a stored current result feeds model fits is decided later, at review acceptance.
