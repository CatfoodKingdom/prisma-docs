Color Model v1

Color Model v1

Jul 24, 20265 min read

Color Model v1 — the transmission model — describes each filament with one measured curve per color channel: transmission as a function of printed thickness. Stacks are predicted by multiplying curves. It is the system’s original color model; Color Model v2 models whole stacks jointly instead.

The profile

A profile is not a fitted formula. A single exponential (Beer-Lambert) does not follow real measurements across thickness — each RGB channel averages a band of wavelengths, which bends the curve, as does scattering in the more opaque filaments — so the model stores the cleaned measurements themselves as knots and interpolates between them with a monotone cubic spline, one per channel. The curve passes exactly through every knot.

Three constraints shape it: transmission is exactly 1 at zero thickness; values clip to [0, 1]; and each channel is forced non-increasing — more material never transmits more light. Beyond the last measured knot the curve continues exponentially, with the decay rate taken from the last two knots.

Composition

A stack’s predicted transmission is the product of its layers’ curves, per channel — order-free, since multiplication commutes. The product law is exact only for narrow bands and pure absorbers (Transmission vs Appearance); the measured residual on real stacks is handled by the pair corrections below.

Where the knots come from

Two kinds of samples feed a filament’s profile:

  • Solo strips — the filament printed alone; each swatch is a direct thickness-and-transmission measurement.
  • White-base strips — the filament printed over a white base. The base is peeled off by dividing every swatch by the strip’s own zero-thickness swatch: a local control from the same print and photograph, canceling the base’s transmission and any exposure difference in one stroke. Below 0.3 mm these win over solo measurements, because ultra-thin solo prints are structurally unreliable.

Strips printed over a colored base are deliberately excluded: the light reaching the filament there is already filtered, so dividing out the base measures absorption of pre-filtered light rather than the broadband response — historically an error near twenty percent for complementary pairs. The app shows these as diagnostics; they never enter a profile.

The merged points are then cleaned: duplicates at the same thickness collapse; interior points deviating more than 35% from the local exponential trend drop as outliers; the zero-thickness anchor is prepended; monotonicity is enforced; values floor at 0.003 so compositions never hard-clip; and the curve truncates one knot past where it sinks under the measurement noise floor (average transmission 0.008).

Which samples count

A filament’s fit uses every accepted processed sample that survives the exclusions above, where it is the variable layer and physically on top; stacks with fixed layers above the variable are skipped rather than reinterpreted. Pending-review extraction results do not enter the production SQLite measurement projection. Exclusion controls are honored throughout — individual swatches, whole samples, or a filament excluded from modeling entirely.

Refitting and use

Fit Models attempts every target profile and then regenerates the corrections when at least one profile succeeded. If one profile fails while another succeeds, the successful subset can become current before the unified workflow reports failure and stops. The current Modeling view does not expose a one-filament refit; lower-level compatibility routes still exist. Upstream changes to a sample — its images, geometry, or filament roles — mark dependent fits stale. Generator never reads these working files directly. After Publish Models copies a complete, validated current set into an immutable library, Generator’s Color Model v1 path reads the copied profiles from its active library and composes stacks with the same product law.

Pair corrections

Multiplying per-filament curves is an approximation: each RGB channel spans a band of wavelengths, and band-averaged transmissions do not multiply exactly (Transmission vs Appearance). Print a real two-filament stack and its measured transmission disagrees with the predicted product. Pair corrections patch that residual empirically, measured pair by measured pair.

What a correction is

For an overlay filament printed on a specific base stack, the correction is the measured-to-predicted ratio per channel, tabulated against overlay thickness:

Cc​(d)=Tpredicted,c​(d)Tmeasured,c​(d)​

Values clamp to [0.3, 3], anchor at exactly 1 at zero thickness, and interpolate linearly between knots, holding constant beyond the last. Channels too dark to trust — predicted transmission under 0.02, or nothing measured — record no correction (a ratio of 1); about a third of stored values are such placeholders. At prediction time the plain composed product is multiplied once by the correction at the overlay’s thickness, looked up by the exact overlay-and-base combination.

Derivation

Corrections regenerate at the tail of a multi-profile fit, from every accepted processed stacked sample — the variable filament over its fixed base layers. Predicted transmission comes from the freshly fitted profiles; each swatch contributes one ratio; duplicate observations at the same thickness average. Single-base and two-base stacks are both measured, and the base’s identity and thickness are part of the correction’s key.

Where they apply — and where they don’t

The table is directional and exact-match: a pair never printed and measured gets no correction, silently. The consumers differ:

  • The calibration app’s own Modeling review view (formerly the Model Fitting subtab) applies the pair corrections to its Color Model v1 predictions by default, and can also show the uncorrected series for comparison.
  • The generator applies them only while building its color lookup table, only to two-filament combinations, and only when its “Color corrections” setting is enabled (the default).
  • In that path the two-base measurements — the majority of the stored table — are dropped, curves measured at different base thicknesses are averaged into one, and when both orderings of a pair exist their factors are averaged too.

The photo stack model replaces this mechanism with a fitted correction layer of its own (Color Model v2).
