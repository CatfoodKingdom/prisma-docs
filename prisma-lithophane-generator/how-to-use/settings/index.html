Settings

Jul 26, 20264 min read

Settings control how Prisma builds the lithophane — its thickness and resolution, any preprocessing applied to the image, how colors are matched, and how the white cap is built. The defaults are sensible: you can make a good print without opening this drawer at all.

The settings drawer

Press the Settings gear (top right) to slide the drawer in from the right — it works from any tab. Pills along the top of the drawer switch between sections: Essentials, Pre-processing, Color Solver, and White Cap. The square button expands the drawer to full width; the × closes it.

Advanced: Off / On (in the drawer header) hides or reveals the less-common settings. Leave it off at first — everything you need day to day is visible without it.

There’s no OK or Apply — changes take hold as you make them, and they apply to the next solve. A solve already running or finished keeps the settings it started with. Exporting an older run uses that run’s frozen settings rather than whatever is currently in the drawer.

Profiles

The bar at the top of the drawer manages settings profiles — named saves of the entire settings collection. The current profile’s name is shown, with a marker when you’ve changed something since it was saved.

  • Profiles… opens the profiles window: pick a profile from the list to load it, or Restore System Defaults to go back to a factory state.
  • Save stores your current settings into the current profile.
  • Save As… stores them under a new name.

Profiles are how you keep useful setups around — for example, a high-detail profile and a quicker draft profile — and switch between them in two clicks. When you find settings that work, save them.

Essentials

The everyday section: the print’s physical envelope and the one big quality choice. The diagram at the top shows the stack these settings describe — white base on the bottom, color layers, white cap on top, viewed from the cap side.

  • Solve Mode — the biggest single choice in the app. Color aims the filament stacks directly at the image’s colors. Luminance lets white-cap thickness carry more of the image’s brightness and fine detail, with color underneath. Compare both when subtle light-and-dark structure matters; the better choice depends on the image and palette.
  • Solve Pitch — the size of one solve point, in millimeters. Smaller = more detail but slower solves and exports. The recommended value follows your active nozzle; there’s little point going finer than the nozzle can print.
  • Max Total Thickness — the print’s total thickness budget. Thicker gives the solver more layers to work with — deeper darks and richer color — at the cost of print time.
  • Layer Height — must exactly match the layer height you’ll slice with. If these disagree, the printed colors won’t match the preview.
  • White Base/Cap Filament — the white used for the solid base and the top cap. This is chosen here, not in the palette (see Palette).
  • Base Thickness — the solid white plate the print sits on.

The line under the table shows what these work out to — how many color layers the budget leaves, and the resulting stack.

The other sections

Each has its own page describing every setting and what it does to the print. The short version:

  • Preprocessing Module Settings — optional clean-up of the photo before solving: noise reduction, smoothing toward printable detail, and reshaping tones and saturation toward what your palette can reproduce. Each module is a simple preset picker; all start off.
  • Color Solver — how colors are matched. The everyday controls: the Appearance Model (the calibrated color prediction — v2 is the default), Color corrections (leave on), Max colors per region (how many filaments may stack in one spot), and Color region target (how fine the color patchwork is).
  • White Cap — the white layer above the colors. Day to day: Min Cap Layers (never below 1) and, in Luminance mode, Shading balance (whether broad shading comes from smooth cap thickness or fine relief).

There is no separate swap-planning setting. When a palette needs more colors than your printer can hold at once, Prisma plans the required filament bands automatically during the solve. See Runs with Swaps for when to use them and how to carry the plan into the slicer.
