The white cap

The white cap

Properties1
tagsgenerator, printability

Jul 26, 20266 min read

In one sentence: the live staged solver chooses a layer-quantized white surface above the committed color ceiling, constrained by minimum cover, total-height and cap budgets, with Color-mode boundary policies or Luminance-mode source guidance; it exports the resulting visible upper surface as a canonical field target.

The cap is not simply “all height left over.” Its thickness is selected within the remaining budget to preserve color appearance, provide a closed white viewing skin, and optionally encode luminance/detail. The white base and cap currently use the same user-selected eligible white filament.

Color ceiling and headroom

For a live staged solve, FillerGeometryPlan.color_ceiling_mm is the per-pixel upper surface of the white base, committed color layers, and any fixed band-white-fill geometry. Stage 4 builds the cap on that surface.

pipeline.height_budget expresses the total-height contract:

  • max_cap_height_for_color_thickness returns t_max - d_wb - color_thickness, optionally limited by a cap maximum and floored at zero.

t_max includes base, color, and cap. The scalar helper is live in Stage 2’s per-stack cap search: over-budget cap steps are assigned infinite cost and cannot be selected. Stage 4 separately computes and enforces its per-pixel remaining budget from the final color ceiling. That Stage 4 calculation remains live and is not routed through the scalar helper.

The ordinary defaults shared by the server/UI config and PipelineConfig are 0.08 mm layer height, 0.20 mm white base, 0.08 mm minimum cap, and 3.0 mm total maximum.

Cap ceilings

PipelineConfig resolves several related values:

  • effective_d_wc_max() uses an explicit cap maximum when present, otherwise t_max - d_wb - d_wc_min;
  • effective_boundary_d_wc_max() begins from that value, then applies any runtime Luminance authority, runtime swap-band cap limit, and explicit boundary_cap_authority_mm, while never returning below d_wc_min; and
  • effective_white_cap() falls back to the white-base filament if a separate internal cap id is absent.

Actual per-pixel headroom still depends on the color ceiling; these scalar accessors are not permission to exceed t_max over a tall color stack.

Color mode

The UI calls the ordinary standard configuration Color mode. Its default Boundary cap is Detail Aware (cap_mode="appearance_bounded_smooth"): Stage 4 keeps a structural boundary surface close to the raw optical solution and carries the remaining desired relief in the detail cap.

boundary_cap_de_budget (UI Appearance budget, default 0.008 dE) limits the model-domain appearance change allowed when the smoothed boundary candidate departs from the raw cap. Pixels that exceed the budget fall back toward the optical cap. The alternate Smooth mode (smooth_variable) allows the whole boundary surface to vary without that structural/residual split. Retired cap_mode="fixed" is rejected by config validation.

The structural split is enabled only for Detail Aware while the Luminance handler is off. In Luminance mode the server/frontend preset forces Smooth, so the structural split cannot run accidentally.

Luminance mode and the handler

The raw PipelineConfig.luminance_handler_enabled default is false. Selecting Luminance in the UI sets luminance_mode="luminance_detail", enables the handler, selects its boundary_ceiling mode, forces Smooth boundary mode, retains mandatory detail/printability gates, and sets the other Luminance preset values. Returning to Color mode through the UI disables the handler.

The handler is configured twice in pipeline.runner:

  1. before LUT construction, to establish a provisional runtime boundary authority; and
  2. after gamut mapping, to rebuild that authority against the final target.

LuminanceHandler.build_reference uses the source/target OKLab L channel, samples the active white-base/white-cap appearance curve over printable layer steps, and produces layer-quantized cap guidance. It uses the configured appearance provider; a requested photo-stack provider is not silently replaced by the spline provider. For a banded solve, fixed white fill is folded into the reference so guidance describes the actual stack.

The internal handler supports several response and blend modes. The product Luminance preset uses boundary_ceiling: broad source shading constrains the boundary request while the remaining desired response can be carried by detail. Other stored modes such as boundary_prior and absolute_optical remain implementation/config surfaces, not separate user Solve Mode buttons.

Boundary authority

The handler derives a scalar optical authority from the white sandwich’s achievable OKLab swing and the configured Shading balance fraction. It can also apply boundary-percentile or explicit-authority limits. That runtime value feeds effective_boundary_d_wc_max, limiting how much height broad boundary shading can consume while leaving the full cap/detail path its own budget.

Stage 4 guidance

LuminanceHandler.build returns:

  • boundary_cap_request_mm, a layer-quantized boundary request limited by the boundary authority; and
  • detail_cap_reference_mm, a per-pixel target at least as high as the boundary request, limited by the full cap maximum and remaining t_max headroom.

When the handler is disabled, Stage 4 instead proceeds from the solver-implied cap and the selected Color-mode boundary policy.

Boundary, detail, and the final visible top

_build_stage4_cap_plan owns the live composition. It resolves the desired boundary surface, optional Detail Aware residual or optical detail candidates, printability authoring/gates, smoothing, and hard budget clamps. The resulting CapSynthesisPlan carries:

  • cap_boundary_top_mm;
  • total cap_height_mm;
  • detail_height_mm; and
  • final_visible_top_mm.

The detail stage is mandatory as a pipeline stage, but Detail depth may be zero and no optional height is forced merely because the stage exists. Both modes author candidate detail under their respective policies, and final printability gates remain mandatory.

Detail-cap smoothing

_apply_stage4_detail_cap_smoothing operates only when all three conditions hold:

  • the Luminance handler is enabled;
  • mandatory detail_cap_enabled is true; and
  • detail_cap_smoothing_enabled is true.

Consequently its default-true switch is a no-op in ordinary Color mode.

The helper converts detail height to whole layer counts, calls smooth_detail_cap_layers, converts back to millimetres, and clamps the result to remaining cap budget and the desired final target. The smoother replaces exact-height speckles, removes small components, and fills small holes in cumulative detail masks. It never modifies color maps or the boundary cap. The current config-derived defaults are a maximum of 1 pixel for exact-height speckles and 2 pixels for cumulative components/holes, with the layer-depth bound supplied by the active Detail depth setting.

Canonical export contract

pipeline.staged_bridge writes the live cap into three thickness-map keys:

  • __white_cap__ for total cap thickness;
  • __white_boundary_cap__; and
  • __white_detail_cap__.

It also exports cap_plan.final_visible_top_mm under white_cap_field_target_upper_surface_map. Metadata schema white-cap-field-target-v1 records the field key, solve/cap modes, required quantized cover floor, effective cap limits, smoothing status, and one of three policies:

  • standard_smooth_variable_canonical;
  • standard_appearance_bounded_structural_split; or
  • luminance_detail_canonical.

Field-derived meshing requires and reconstructs from this target. Exact raster uses total cap thickness directly but still consumes the same explicit physical/mode metadata. Export does not guess a current cap from dormant helpers.

User-facing controls

The supported UI controls are:

  • Solve Mode: Color or Luminance;
  • Max Total Thickness and Layer Height in Essentials;
  • Min Cap Layers;
  • Boundary cap: Detail Aware or Smooth (hidden/forced Smooth in Luminance);
  • Appearance budget for Detail Aware;
  • Smoothing radius;
  • Shading balance in Luminance; and
  • Detail depth.

Internal flags and ceilings described above are useful for understanding persisted configs and the solver contract, but they are not all independent controls in the current drawer.
