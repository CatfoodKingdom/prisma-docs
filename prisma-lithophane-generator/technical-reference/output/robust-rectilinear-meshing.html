Robust rectilinear meshing

Robust rectilinear meshing

Properties1
tagsgenerator, meshing

Jul 28, 20269 min read

In one sentence: Prisma converts grid-aligned vertical material intervals into triangle shells with sector-aware contact topology, repair and strict welded-quality checks, then partitions a failing body into same-material quarantine or Z parts while preserving any remaining failure in the export report.

This page covers the live geometry engine under generator/mesh/: the interval mesher used by every color and exact-raster cap, the field-derived overlap cap, and the export-level fallback ladder.

Production call graph

The browser’s background export reaches _perform_export_files → export_solve_bundle. Both geometry sources call _base_and_color_objects for the flat base and exact solve-grid color stack.

Geometry sourceLive white-cap path
exact_rasterbuild_exact_raster_mesh_bundle → mesh_interval_map, with quarantine/adaptive fallbacks
field_derivedfield reconstruction → build_field_derived_mesh_bundle_mixed_resolution → build_overlap_white_cap_mesh

build_overlap_white_cap_mesh is the live field-cap builder. It constructs rigid-guard and freeform submeshes, lowers the freeform floor by one printed layer at their contact cells, concatenates them into one physical white-cap MeshObject, and reports the component and combined quality. The older build_hybrid_white_cap_meshes function still emits separate rigid/freeform objects and remains covered by tests, but the product exporter does not call it.

Some source comments still call the overlap route a prototype. Callers and tests, not that historical adjective, establish its production status.

mesh_interval_map: vertical interval to shell

mesh_interval_map(floor, thickness, pitch_mm, ...) takes two equal H×W arrays in millimetres and returns a trimesh.Trimesh plus frozen RectilinearMeshStats. Each active XY column represents the half-open vertical material interval from floor to floor + thickness.

The function accepts a cooperative cancel_check, and its expensive construction/repair loops call it at bounded checkpoints.

Snapped Z levels and slab occupancy

When z_snap_decimals is set, floor and top values are rounded through integer units and top is clamped not below floor. Export uses six decimal places. Snapping makes nominally shared material boundaries exactly equal.

All distinct active floor/top heights become one global Z-level array. Consecutive levels define slabs, and _occupancy_by_slab uses vectorized searchsorted indices to produce an H×W×K occupied-cell grid. A thickness at or below eps is inactive.

Exposed faces

_neighbor_masks shifts occupancy in six directions. A face is exposed wherever an occupied voxel-slab has an empty neighbour. _append_quads records each face by its four (row, column, z-level, local-cell) corners; each quad becomes two triangles.

The Y coordinate is inverted during world-coordinate reconstruction so the mesh uses image-up orientation. X and Y are multiples of pitch_mm; Z comes from the snapped level table.

The 2×2×2 sector rule

At one grid corner, as many as eight occupied voxel-slabs can meet. _corner_patterns packs that neighbourhood into an 8-bit occupancy code. SECTOR_LOOKUP precomputes, for all 256 codes, the six-connected components among the occupied local cells. SECTOR_COMPONENT_COUNT records how many components a pattern contains.

A face vertex is keyed by (row, column, z-level, sector), not only its geometric coordinate. Therefore two same-material regions that touch only at a point or edge receive distinct topological vertices at the same location. Without this split, an STL reader could treat the contact as one edge/point incident to too many faces and create a non-manifold pinch.

The encoded integer key incorporates grid row/column, Z-level index, and one of eight sector slots. Unique keys produce the vertex array and inverse indices produce faces. The raw trimesh is constructed with process=False, then duplicate and degenerate faces are dropped.

Optional horizontal-face merging

Export enables merge_horizontal_faces. Side walls remain cell-aligned quads, but top and bottom masks are triangulated by connected plateau rather than emitting a pair of triangles for every cell.

The merge path:

  1. labels four-connected components at each exposed horizontal slab plane;
  2. traces exact grid boundary rings with trace_mask_grid_rings;
  3. groups outer rings and holes by orientation/containment;
  4. triangulates with Shapely constrained Delaunay triangles, falling back to mapbox_earcut; and
  5. maps every polygon vertex back to the same sector-aware integer key used by side walls.

Ring coordinates deliberately retain collinear grid vertices. Simplifying them would let a large horizontal triangle edge skip vertices used by the cell-aligned wall and could open the shell.

The unmerged route remains supported. Because it uses only sector-keyed cell quads, it skips the initial overfull-fan repair that merged triangulation may need.

Repair pipeline

After raw construction, the interval mesher performs these operations in order:

  1. Split overfull edge fans on the merged-horizontal route. At edges used by more than two faces, _split_overfull_edge_fans separates incident connected face fans by cloning vertices.
  2. Stitch paired duplicate open edges. _stitch_duplicate_open_edges groups geometrically identical open edges at nine decimal places and union-welds only a group containing exactly two edges.
  3. Re-split overfull fans after stitching on the merged route.
  4. Separate remaining coincident topological vertices. _separate_duplicate_vertices moves duplicate vertices by a tiny XY-only offset toward their incident-face centroid.
  5. Fill residual holes, for at most two passes. Added faces receive another fan split and, when a split occurred, duplicate-vertex separation.
  6. Fix normals when requested.

Cancellation checkpoints exist before, between, and within the large vector/group loops.

Why coincident vertices are separated

STL stores triangles, not persistent vertex identity. A slicer may weld different vertices that have identical coordinates, undoing the sector split. Export’s default split_vertex_offset_mm is 0.001 mm. The offset changes X/Y only; Z and the layer planes remain unchanged. It introduces a minute physical separation exactly at already topologically separate touch contacts so a reload cannot recreate the pinch.

The freeform-cap mesher has an equivalent contact-separation step for its own diagonal corner cases.

Quality gates

check_mesh_quality counts unique-edge face references and reports:

  • vertices/faces;
  • watertightness;
  • winding consistency;
  • open edges (one incident face); and
  • pinch edges (other non-two-valence edges).

_quality_passes requires watertight, zero open edges, and zero pinch edges.

Rectilinear color and cap acceptance also uses _strict_processed_quality. It rebuilds a copy with trimesh(..., process=True), intentionally allowing normal vertex welding, then requires watertightness and zero non-two/open edges. Passing the first check but failing this one means the object is clean in its identity-preserving form but likely to pinch after a slicer-style weld.

Fallbacks are attempts, not a blanket success guarantee. Any emitted part that still fails remains in the bundle’s quality map and makes the final manifest mesh_quality_failed.

Topology-risk quarantine

topology_risk_column_mask uses the same snapped interval occupancy and SECTOR_COMPONENT_COUNT table to mark XY columns adjacent to multi-sector corners. _build_interval_topology_quarantine_parts tries six deterministic subsets of those columns:

  • even/odd rows;
  • even/odd columns; and
  • even/odd checkerboards.

For each candidate it meshes a main body with those cells removed and a quarantine body containing only those cells. The first candidate for which all nonempty parts pass both quality gates becomes ready. If there are no risk columns, it reports no_risk_columns; if all candidates fail, it returns the final candidate and a mesh_quality_failed report so the caller can escalate.

The split preserves which interval-map cells belong to the material and both objects retain the original material key. Each part still goes through the ordinary reload-safety separation, so the same deliberate 0.001 mm XY contact gaps can appear at topologically split vertices.

Exact-raster cap fallback

The ordinary exact-cap sequence is:

  1. one combined interval body;
  2. topology quarantine; and
  3. adaptive Z partition.

The internal force_exact_white_cap_slabs switch bypasses that decision and immediately emits constant-Z slabs; it defaults false and is not an HTTP export option.

Adaptive partitioning builds the whole snapped Z range first. A passing range is accepted as one part. A failing range spanning multiple level gaps is bisected recursively. A failing one-gap leaf cannot split further, so it is still emitted with role white_cap_slab_failed and included in leaf_failures. Export thereby preserves all intended geometry and makes the unresolved defect explicit rather than dropping material.

Color fallback

For each ordered color material, _base_and_color_objects tries:

  1. one interval body;
  2. topology quarantine; then
  3. one constant-Z body per occupied snapped level gap.

Every slab records ordinary and strict quality. Failed slabs are still emitted and reported. The white base does not use this ladder; it is one flat box from the build plate through d_wb_mm.

Live field-derived overlap cap

derive_hybrid_white_cap_intervals divides reconstructed white material into a rigid guard domain near color and a freeform domain elsewhere. The guard sits on color and extends laterally according to the GeometryPrintabilityPolicy. validate_hybrid_white_cap_intervals checks layer occupancy for exposed color and removed reference-white coverage.

build_overlap_white_cap_mesh then:

  • meshes the rigid guard through mesh_interval_map;
  • verifies exact guard/freeform contact;
  • lowers the freeform floor by one layer at contact cells, refusing to cross below the white-base top;
  • meshes the freeform top/bottom skins and walls;
  • concatenates both submeshes without a boolean union; and
  • returns one __white_cap__ object with component and combined quality.

The deliberate same-material overlap is what lets slicers receive one cap object without requiring an expensive CAD boolean. The object’s metadata records contact count, maximum contact delta, overlap depth, each component’s quality/strict-edge summary, and the combined result.

HybridWhiteCapMeshResult.status is mesh_quality_failed for any recorded open/pinch defect, coverage_review when interval coverage did not pass, otherwise ready.

Field reconstruction upstream

reconstruct_field_white_cap_from_arrays validates the canonical target top surface against the solve-grid color ceiling, bilinearly resizes both to the selected integer field scale, and reconstructs white occupancy one printed Z layer at a time.

For each layer, _cleanup_white_mask:

  • fills four-connected internal air holes smaller than the policy threshold;
  • removes four-connected positive-white components smaller than its threshold; and
  • removes any white/color overlap.

Thresholds convert solve-grid-derived physical areas to fine-grid pixel counts. The result is an integer-layer thickness map plus a detailed per-layer/total audit; mesh construction happens later.

Policy and audit records

GeometryPrintabilityPolicy defaults its minimum printable width and guard width to solve-grid pitch, maximum internal empty-hole area to 0.5 × width², and minimum positive feature area to width². A finer field mesh does not relax those solve-owned physical thresholds.

RectilinearMeshStats records active columns, Z levels/slabs, quads, vertices/faces, sector splits, fan splits, stitched open edges, separated vertices, filled faces, horizontal-merge totals, topology-risk summary, snap precision, and build time. Export copies these into per-object/fallback reports, together with the rejected single-body ordinary and strict quality summaries.
